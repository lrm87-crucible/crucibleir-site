Vendor-neutral vs. marketplace-tied surplus asset software

Many surplus asset platforms come from companies whose main business is selling surplus, through their own marketplace, auctions, or resale operations. That can be a good fit for some teams. It also shapes what the software is built to do.

The differences that matter

Marketplace-tied platformsVendor-neutral software (Crucible)
Who earns money when an asset sellsThe platform’s operator often earns commissions, fees, or resale margin.Only you. Crucible charges a subscription and takes no part of a sale.
Where assets are soldUsually through the operator’s own marketplace or auctions.Anywhere you choose: your own buyers, brokers, or any marketplace.
Redeployment between sitesVaries by vendor.Built in: a window, requests, approvals, and purchases-avoided reporting.
How you payOften bundled with services or tied to sales volume.A simple annual subscription per company.
Best suited toTeams that want a provider to run sales end to end.Teams that want to run the process themselves with a clear, current picture.

These describe general patterns across the category, not any single vendor. Every product differs, so check the specifics with each one.

When a marketplace-tied platform may be the better choice

If your team is small and you want someone else to market and sell equipment on your behalf, a full-service provider can make sense. Software alone won’t find buyers for you. Crucible is designed for teams that have or want control over the sale itself.

Questions to ask any vendor

  1. Who earns money when one of our assets sells?
  2. Can we sell through other channels without penalty?
  3. Who owns our data, and can we export all of it at any time?
  4. Is our data used for any other business, including buying or reselling?
  5. What happens to our records if we leave?
  6. How are approvals and compliance checks handled before an asset is listed?
